Conclusions

A few unique observations fell out of this study. We'll list them below.



Effects that spanned both heterogeneous and homogeneous gender dyads

The Ventral Attention Network (in results: VAT)

Greater differences in the Ventral Attention Network predicts a decrease in convergence-entropy. This indicates that, preceding greater lexico-semantic alignment, individuals first disalign in their neurological states in this region. This effect holds true whether individuals are of the same gender, or in mixed gender dyads.



Effects specicific to heterogeneous gender dyads

The Dorsal Attention Network (in results: DAN)

Greater differences in the Ventral Attention Network predict an increase in convergence-entropy, but only in mixed gender dyads. In other words, if participants in mixed gender dyads diverge in fNIRS values in the DAT, then lexico-semantic convergence drops. The greater the difference in mixed gender dyads, the less that participants are discussing the same ideas.


The Right Superior Parietal Lobule (in results: R_SPL)

Greater differences in the Right Superior Parietal Lobule (rSPL) predict an increase in convergence-entropy, but only in mixed gender dyads. In other words, if participants in mixed gender dyads diverge in fNIRS values in the rSPL, then lexico-semantic convergence drops. Again, if people diverge in their neural measurements here, then they're unlikely to be talking about the same ideas when talking to one another.


The Left Temporoparietal Juncture (in results: L_TPJ)

Greater differences in the Left Temporoparietal Juncture predict a decrease in convergence-entropy. Like what we see in the VAT generally, preceding greater lexico-semantic alignment individuals disalign in their neurological states in this region, but only in the heterogeneous gender condition.



A lack of coherence in other regions/networks means . . . what?

So what does it mean that for a number of networks and a number of regions they had (1) no coherence, and thus (2) the cumulative effects of adding all the fNIRS channels that are contained within their boundaries are inconclusive? That depends. Our analysis here effectively tested whether or not all the channels in a pre-defined boundary, be that a network or a coritcal region, worked together in tandem to produce a strong effect. The null results reported on here can represent a couple different things from that perspective.

One possibility is that the channels in that boundary are indeed entirely chaotic, and thus the null result we found is a true null result. That could mean that in other studies where folks report on more aggregate statistics per region, that their results may not actually represent the region as a whole operating cohesively, but they could be reporting on potentially strong indicators from a small subset of possible channels or regions. The network, the region--these aren't the loci of results. Some smaller, more granular areas of activation are what power people's findings.

Another possible, exciting, and equally likely possibility is that there are sub network and subregion regularities. Think of this like cortical regions having a rather extreme degree of substructure. One can modify the methods we've used in this study in order to potentially identify those substructures, as well. If one tested, for all the channels in a region, all the permuted combinations of channels associated with that region, one could identify potential substructures in networks or regions that actually do exhibit coherent effects. While we do not do that in the current study, it is indeed possible (though note that the burden of proof for that sort of exploratory analysis should be $\alpha / k_{permutations}$).
